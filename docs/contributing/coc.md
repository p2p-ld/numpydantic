# Code of Conduct

## Baseline Acceptable Behavior

With respect to a baseline of civil discourse, 
we import the language of the [Contributor Covenenant 3.0](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/3/0/code_of_conduct/).
Most of that shit should not need to be said,
but open source developers constantly love to probe the bottom of acceptable norms for no reason.

This is a project with very little development or moderation resources available to it.
Accordingly, it uses a BDFL moderation system until it needs anything better:
Jonny will wield the banhammer as needed,
and reports can be made by emailing me 
(sneakers-the-rat [the normal symbol that's here]  protonmail [the other normal symbol here] com)
or DMing me on [fedi](https://neuromatch.social/@jonny).

## Labor

Labor will always be properly attributed, giving maximal possible credit.
The maintainers will go out of their way to work with you to land a PR,
rather than re-writing it themselves and leaving your contributions stranded.
The changelog will always reflect the author of a PR, as will the commit history.

## The Prickliness Of Specs

Specifications tend to attract people with um precise and orderly minds,
with lots of very specific and esoteric opinions,
and often the negotiations betwen people with this set of traits are less than civil.
The python typing spec has a lot of quirks, strange behaviors, 
and reasonable disagreements can be had in the way things should be done - 
this package attempts to balance adherence to the specifications with usability,
and this often buts up against people who land on either side of those poles.

Disagreements about the structure of the package and its spec-alignment are welcome!
But we insist that such discussions maintain basic respect and decorum,
and will not permit "spec bullying" where arcane knowledge of the python spec
is used to exclude, demean, degrade, or put down another participant.

## No Bad Questions

We accept all feature requests, even those made naively,
and will do our best to accomodate them against the background limitations of the python spec.
We will never close an issue raised in good faith without discussion unless it is spam,
and when a feature request is impossible,
we will do our best to explain that rather than dismissing it out of hand.

Someone should never feel shy or embarassed to raise an issue,
all good-faith questions and requests are welcome,
and the maintainers will do all they can to avoid ever making someone feel unwelcome or lesser-than.
